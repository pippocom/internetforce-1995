# Google Groups — public Usenet traces

This directory preserves screenshots and references to historical Usenet
content that remains accessible through the public Google Groups archive.

## Internet Force accounts in newsgroups

A Google Groups search for `Internetforce.com`, performed in October 2026,
displayed **208 conversations**. The screenshots preserved here show 1996
results containing addresses under the `internetforce.com` and `intf.com`
domains across very different newsgroups, including `rec.scuba`,
`comp.os.ms-windows...`, `az.teens`, `rec.arts.anime.music` and
`comp.dcom.sys.cisco`.

The figure 208 is the number of conversations displayed by the search at the
time of capture: **it is not a count of distinct users or accounts**. The
presence of these addresses in groups unrelated to the provider is consistent
with ordinary subscriber use of Usenet, in addition to any staff activity.

Files:

- `google-groups-search-internetforce-com-01.png`
- `google-groups-search-internetforce-com-02.png`
- `google-groups-search-internetforce-com-03.png`

The visible addresses document Usenet participation. Attributing a particular
message's NNTP path to a specific news server requires the message's full
headers, such as `Path` and `NNTP-Posting-Host`; that check is kept separate
from the sender address domain alone.

## Technical GSM and SMS post — 22 March 1996

The screenshot `marco-gsm-sms-1996.jpeg` preserves a message posted by
**Marco Iannacone** on **22 March 1996** in `alt.cellular-phone-tech`.

In the message Iannacone identifies himself as Internet Force's System
Administrator and asks for technical information to build a free Italian
service linking e-mail and GSM phones through SMS. It is a contemporary primary
trace of technical work carried out while Internet Force was operating.

Google Groups message:

<https://groups.google.com/g/alt.cellular-phone-tech/c/2IOGEvXaQwA/m/8CqAQtnX0TYJ>

The screenshot shows the public address `ianna@iol.it`. I had used that address
since 1994 and I often used it in public newsgroups, also to avoid routinely exposing primary
mailboxes.

## Technical continuity after Internet Force — PHP, 1998

A **1998** thread in `ufra.lists.php` documents Iannacone's continuing technical
activity after Internet Force: in the discussion he refers to earlier PHP
experience at several ISPs and participates in a thread involving **Rasmus
Lerdorf**, the creator of PHP.

<https://groups.google.com/g/ufra.lists.php/c/1AgRCdPNnaE/m/eJ7MSY4dnXYJ>

The thread belongs to the immediate post-Internet-Force technical context and is
kept here as a public reference connected with the professional trajectory
documented by the archive.
