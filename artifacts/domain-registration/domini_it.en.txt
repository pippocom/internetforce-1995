Registration of .it domains - Internet Force procedure 1995-1996
================================================================

This document reconstructs how Internet Force registered Italian .it domains in
the years 1995-1996. The reconstruction is based on the email exchanges with
GARR-NIS (the service that managed the .it registry at the time) and on the DNS
configurations of the period.

Compared with .com/.net domains, registering a .it domain required two things
together:

- technical DNS requirements: a configured zone and declared nameservers;
- administrative information about the requesting subject, with a declaration
  of assumption of responsibility also sent by fax.

The registry was not InterNIC but GARR-NIS (GARR Network Information Service,
c/o CNUCE - Istituto del CNR, Pisa). The request was not submitted through an
InterNIC template but as a RIPE-style textual object, sent by email.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Who did what
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Customer
  Provided the domain name, the company/body data and the administrative
  contacts.

Internet Force (Marco Iannacone, with the technical and administrative contacts)
  Collected the data, registered the organization, prepared the DNS zone, filled
  in the domain object (with the nameservers) and sent it to GARR-NIS, handled
  the declaration of responsibility by fax and followed the exchanges through to
  completion.

GARR-NIS (.it registry)
  Received the requests at domain@nis.garr.it, the registration of organizations
  at ORG-REG@NIS.GARR.IT and the exchanges with staff@nis.garr.it. The system
  had two automatic phases and one human verification phase: a "syntax check
  phase" (syntactic check by a robot) and a "semantic control phase"
  (verification of the domain name and cross-check with the documents of the
  requesting organization), which could take up to 10 working days.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Workflow followed by Internet Force
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From the preserved exchanges this sequence emerges:

1.  Request for the domain and collection of the requester's data.
2.  Registration of the organization with GARR-NIS
    (message to ORG-REG@NIS.GARR.IT with the company data and the contacts).
3.  Preparation of the domain object in RIPE style: domain name, organization,
    description, administrative/technical/postmaster/zone contacts, nameservers,
    network, remark, maintainer.
4.  DNS configuration: zone on the Internet Force server and declared
    nameservers (Internet Force primary, GARR secondary dns.nis.garr.it).
5.  Sending the request to domain@nis.garr.it.
6.  Automatic syntactic check ("Syntax Check Phase") by the GARR-NIS robot. If
    there are no errors, the request passes the phase.
7.  Passing to the "semantic control phase" by the registry staff.
8.  Sending by fax of the declaration of assumption of responsibility and of the
    detailed information about the company requested for registration.
9.  Handling of any warnings or correction requests and, if needed, re-sending of
    the request.
10. Completion: GARR-NIS confirms with "Update OK: [domain] ..." and sends a
    notification of the new object created in the database.
11. Postmaster reachability test: GARR sends an automatic message to
    postmaster@<domain> to check that the address works, as foreseen by the rules
    subscribed at the time of registration.

Note on the maintainer. From spring-summer 1996 GARR-NIS made the mnt-by
attribute (reference to the object's "maintainer") mandatory in domain objects:
in the absence of that field the robot prevented the request from passing the
syntactic check. Internet Force used the maintainer INTERNETFORCE-MNT.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Data requested
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Domain field (RIPE-style "domain" object):

  domain        name of the domain (e.g. cnn.it)
  x400-domain   X.400 representation (e.g. c=it; admd=0; prmd=cnn;)
  org           organization holding the domain
  descr         description of the organization
  admin-c       administrative contact
  tech-c        technical contact (one or more)
  postmaster    address responsible for mail
  zone-c        contact for the DNS zone
  nserver       nameserver, with IP address (one or more)
  dom-net       network associated with the domain
  remarks       notes (e.g. "Fully-managed")
  mnt-by        maintainer responsible for the object (e.g. INTERNETFORCE-MNT)
  changed       author and date of the last modification
  source        source of the data (GARR-NIS)

Related "person" objects (one for each contact):

  person, address (street, city, postal code, country), phone, fax-no, e-mail,
  changed, source

Registration of the organization (separate message):

  organization-name, organization-alias-name, organization-unit,
  address-street, address-locality, address-province, address-zip, country,
  descr, phone, fax-no, telex, associated-domain, dir-admin, directory,
  and the related person objects

Administrative data.
  From the exchanges it emerges that to complete the registration an "assumption
  of responsibility" was necessary: Marco Iannacone had sent by fax "la mia
  domanda di assunzione di responsabilita', e le informazioni dettagliate sulla
  nostra societa' che richiedete per la registrazione di un dominio" ["my letter
  of assumption of responsibility, and the detailed information about our
  company that you request for the registration of a domain"]. The preserved
  emails do not list in detail the individual company data contained in that fax;
  therefore fields that are not documented are not reported here.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Brief explanation of the DNS concepts
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SOA, NS, A, MX, CNAME, primary/master DNS, secondary/slave DNS and delegation
are the same concepts described for .com/.net domains in the document
domini_com_net.txt. In the .it case two elements typical of this registry are
added:

- the secondary nameserver was often the GARR DNS (dns.nis.garr.it,
  131.114.2.5 / 192.12.192.5), which appeared as an NS of the zone;
- the delegation took place through the GARR-NIS registry, which published the
  nameservers declared in the domain object.

For .it domains too the close relationship between registration and DNS holds:
the zone had to be ready and the nameservers reachable, otherwise the domain
would not resolve even once registered.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Real example of a request (domain object)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Below is the domain object sent for cnn.it (mailbox GARR-DOMINI_IT.mailbox), in
the final version that passed the checks. The structure and terminology are the
original ones.

    domain:    cnn.it
    x400-domain: c=it; admd=0; prmd=cnn;
    org:       Cyber National Network
    descr:     Cyber National Network - Servizi su Internet
    admin-c:   Christian Colombo
    tech-c:    Marco Iannacone
    tech-c:    Christian Colombo
    postmaster: Christian Colombo
    zone-c:    Christian Colombo
    nserver:   206.20.228.70  cnn-server.cnn.it
    nserver:   131.114.2.5  192.12.192.5  dns.nis.garr.it
    nserver:   206.20.95.3 dns.internetforce.it
    dom-net:   206.20.228.0
    remarks:   Fully-managed
    mnt-by:    INTERNETFORCE-MNT
    changed:   ianna@marco.internetforce.com 960902
    source:    GARR-NIS

The outcome, reported by GARR-NIS, was:

    Syntax Check Phase OK: [domain] cnn.it
    ...
    Update OK: [domain] cnn.it

followed by the "OBJECT BELOW CREATED" notification with the new domain object.

The same exchange also documents the registration of the organization "Cyber
National Network" and of the related person objects (Marco Iannacone and
Christian Colombo), which were sent separately to ORG-REG@NIS.GARR.IT.

Domain internetforce.it.
  The request for internetforce.it (June 1996) shows the complete case with
  registration of the organization and of the zone before completion; the domain
  is declared "Fully-managed" with nameservers dns.internetforce.it
  (206.20.95.3) and dns.nis.garr.it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Real example of a DNS configuration (.it)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The domain promotion.it, registered with GARR-NIS, is hosted on the Internet
Force DNS. The generated zone is in
systems/data/dns/named-data/primary/promotion.it:

    @  IN  SOA  dns.promotion.it. dnsmaster.promotion.it. (
              1996092404   ; Serial
              86400        ; Refresh
              3600         ; Retry 1 hour
              604800       ; Expire after a week
              86400 )      ; Minimum ttl 1 day
                        NS  dns.nis.garr.it.
                        NS  dns.promotion.it.
    www     A   206.20.95.143
    dns     A   206.20.95.3
    localhost  A  127.0.0.1

Here too the zone mirrors the registration:

- the nameservers declared to GARR (dns.promotion.it and dns.nis.garr.it) appear
  as NS records;
- the host www has the site's address (206.20.95.143).

The complete zone: systems/data/dns/named-data/primary/promotion.it
Master source: systems/data/dns/named-data/promotion.it.source
Declaration in named.boot: systems/data/dns/named.boot (line "primary
promotion.it").

Another example linked to the exchanges: the domain cnn.it appears as a
secondary zone managed by Internet Force
(systems/data/dns/named-data/secondary/cnn.it; source cnn.it.source), with the
primary at CNN (cnn-server.cnn.it, 206.20.228.70).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. Errors and iterations
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The process was manual and provided for several check points.

- Syntax warning. In the first request for internetforce.it the GARR robot
  reported:

      WARNING:     if you have a mntner object, please add the "mnt-by:" tag.

  Since the message added that "Objects that just generated a WARNING have been
  reset to the correct value and have gotten over the syntax control phase",
  Marco asked the staff to confirm that the request did not have to be
  re-sent. It is a concrete example of how the automatic syntactic check could
  report a missing field without definitively blocking the request.

- Declaration of responsibility. Before completion, the letter of assumption of
  responsibility was sent by fax together with the information about the
  company. Registration therefore required an administrative step on paper, not
  only electronic.

- Re-sends and updates. For promotion.it one observes a first send, a positive
  syntactic check and then a later message with the subject "Re: registrazione
  dominio promotion.it (2)" ["Re: promotion.it domain registration (2)"] that
  produces the "OBJECT BELOW CREATED" notification with the nameserver updated
  to dns.promotion.it.

- Unrecognized domains. GARR-NIS periodically sent the "Lista dei domini non
  riconosciuti" ["List of unrecognized domains"] and flagged the domains that
  remained without an associated maintainer, marked with
  "mnt-by: NO-PROVIDER-MNT" and deactivatable after six months. It helps to
  understand how central the maintainer attribute was in the .it registry.
