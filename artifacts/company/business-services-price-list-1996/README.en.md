# Internet Force Business Price List — 15 June 1996

> Original Internet Force document from 1996, preserved and supplied in 2026 by **Pascal Schait**, a founding shareholder of Internet Force.

This directory preserves the Internet Force **Business Price List** dated **15 June 1996**. It shows that the ISP's commercial offer extended well beyond individual dial-up access and included business services: static IP addresses, LAN connectivity, leased lines, domain registration, DNS, Web-page production and hosting.

## What it documents

The price list includes, among other services:

- **Static IP Address**, routed on the Internet Force network;
- **LAN IP Dial-Up** for 4, 8 or an unlimited number of PCs;
- **19.2 Kbps** and **64 Kbps LAN IP** connections;
- **Domain Name** registration and Domain Name Server backup;
- HTML page creation and maintenance;
- Web space on Internet Force servers;
- specialised technical support.

Prices are stated in **Italian lire** and, except for items marked as one-off charges, are annual. The document also states 19% VAT and activation times of roughly 15–20 working days for several connectivity services.

A few examples make the commercial scale of the period concrete:

| Service | Listed price |
|---|---:|
| Static IP | Lit. 500,000 activation + Lit. 1,600,000/year |
| LAN IP Dial-Up, 4 PCs | Lit. 2,000,000/year |
| LAN IP Dial-Up, 8 PCs | Lit. 3,500,000/year |
| LAN IP Dial-Up, unlimited | Lit. 5,000,000/year |
| 19.2 Kbps LAN IP | Lit. 1,500,000 activation + Lit. 10,000,000/year |
| 64 Kbps LAN IP | Lit. 1,500,000 activation + Lit. 21,000,000/year |
| HTML page production | Lit. 150,000/hour; Lit. 500,000/4h; Lit. 800,000/8h |
| Domain Name registration | Lit. 500,000 one-off |
| Domain Name Server backup | Lit. 500,000/year |
| 1 / 5 / 10 MB Web space | Lit. 1,000,000 / 4,000,000 / 6,000,000 per year |

## How large was a business website in 1996?

The price list offered Web space in **1 MB, 5 MB and 10 MB** tiers; quantities above 10 MB were **priced on request**. In 1996 these sizes matched the scale of the business Web of the period: for many small companies, an online presence could consist of a few presentation pages, contact details and a contact form, perhaps with a small product catalogue.

Website size was also constrained by the visitor's network. Many users connected through **28.8 kbit/s modems**: pages containing large, high-quality images could require very long transfer times. Server storage and access bandwidth therefore pushed in the same direction, towards sites that were small, lightweight and much more text-oriented than contemporary sites.

One detail captures the scale particularly well: **10 MB was already the largest Web-space tier with a published price; anything larger required a quotation**.
