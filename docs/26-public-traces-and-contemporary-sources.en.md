# Public traces and contemporary sources

> **Internet Force 1995–1996 Historical Archive**
> Historical reconstruction and technical documentation based on original Internet Force materials preserved by Marco Iannacone.
> Author and archive curator: Marco Iannacone · https://pippo.com
> License for the text of this page: [CC BY 4.0](../LICENSE)

The Internet Force reconstruction combines original configurations and
documents, direct first-person testimony from the person who designed and ran
the infrastructure, material preserved by other participants, and traces that
remain in public archives.

This page brings together several public sources from the operating period or
the years immediately following it. They do not replace the technical artifacts
and do not establish a hierarchy between source types: they document different
parts of the same history and allow facts from different contexts to be
cross-checked.

## Usenet: Internet Force accounts in use in 1996

Google Groups preserves many 1996 conversations containing addresses under the
`internetforce.com` and `intf.com` domains.

A search for `Internetforce.com`, displays **208 conversations**. 
The screenshots show Internet Force addresses in very different
newsgroups, including groups about scuba diving, Windows, teenagers, anime and
Cisco. Their distribution across subjects unrelated to the provider is
consistent with ordinary subscriber use of Usenet, in addition to any staff
activity.

→ [Google Groups screenshots and references](../artifacts/usenet/google-groups/README.en.md)

Sender addresses document public account use. Establishing whether a specific
message travelled through the Internet Force news server requires inspection of
the full message headers, particularly fields such as `Path` and
`NNTP-Posting-Host`.

## A contemporary technical post — 22 March 1996

On 22 March 1996 Marco Iannacone posted to `alt.cellular-phone-tech` asking for
technical information about linking e-mail and GSM phones through SMS and about
building a free service in Italy.

In the message he identifies himself as **Internet Force's System
Administrator**. The post is valuable as a public primary source written while
the provider was operating: it places technical activity in its contemporary
context rather than relying only on a reconstruction written decades later.

→ [GSM/SMS post and Google Groups URL](../artifacts/usenet/google-groups/README.en.md#technical-gsm-and-sms-post--22-march-1996)

## Later technical continuity — PHP in 1998

A 1998 Google Groups thread in `ufra.lists.php` shows Iannacone discussing
earlier PHP experience at several ISPs in a conversation involving **Rasmus
Lerdorf**, the creator of PHP.

The thread post-dates Internet Force and belongs to the immediately subsequent
technical context, when Iannacone continued working with Internet providers and
infrastructure.

→ [1998 PHP thread reference](../artifacts/usenet/google-groups/README.en.md#technical-continuity-after-internet-force--php-1998)

## `internetforce.com` on the Wayback Machine — 5 June 1997

The earliest identified Wayback capture of `internetforce.com` is dated
**5 June 1997**, after the central-services migration had already been
completed. The page shows **Enter's** website.

The snapshot therefore documents a public trace of the post-migration phase:
the `internetforce.com` domain was already being used within the Enter
environment to which the central services and remaining Milan customers had
been moved.

→ [Wayback snapshot of internetforce.com](../artifacts/web-archive/README.en.md#internetforcecom--5-june-1997)

For the technical cutover reconstruction see
[The closure of Internet Force and the later evolutions](17-wind-down-and-migrations.en.md).

## `pesaro.com` on the Wayback Machine — 3 May 1997

The **3 May 1997** capture shows **Pesaro Point** already operating
independently, while still displaying the **Internet Force I.S.P.** mark next to
the Pesaro.com/Pesaro Point logo. The same page advertises Internet
connectivity and includes a **“Guida ad Internet” curated by Marco Iannacone**.

It is a public trace of continuity between the former Pesaro POP and the later
independent activity, consistent with the post-Internet-Force configurations and
documents preserved in the repository.

→ [Wayback snapshot of pesaro.com](../artifacts/web-archive/README.en.md#pesarocom--3-may-1997)

## Business Register and Via Plana 20

The public Italian Business Register record shows Internet Force S.r.l. as a
**Milan local unit** at **Via Plana Giovanni Antonio 20**.

The same address appears in the Business Price List dated 15 June 1996 as
**“Sede Operativa: Via Plana, 20, Milano”**. The consulting agreement dated
2 January 1996 instead gives the company's registered office in Pandino (CR):
the documents therefore distinguish the legal/registered office from the
operating Milan location.

The current record also contains an activity description including data
transmission and reception. The ATECO code displayed today is not used to
reconstruct the company's 1995–1996 administrative classification: ATECO '91
did not yet contain a specific category for Internet access provision as an ISP;
such an entry appears explicitly in ATECO 2002.

→ [Public Business Register record](../artifacts/company/company-registry/README.en.md)

## How these sources are used

These traces are most useful when they connect different layers of the
reconstruction:

- technical files show **how** systems, services and networks were configured;
- commercial documents show **what** was sold and at what scale;
- company records show addresses, roles and legal transitions;
- Usenet shows people and accounts using the public network;
- the Wayback Machine preserves visible fragments of the subsequent phases;
- my first-person testimony reconstructs decisions, procedures and sequences that
  did not always leave a formal document; where present, the testimonies of other participants are indicated separately.

The value of the archive lies in the convergence of different source types, not
in requiring every historical detail to have an identical duplicate in an
external archive.
