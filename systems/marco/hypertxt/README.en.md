# Hypertxt — recovered material

> **Internet Force 1995–1996 Historical Archive**
> Historical reconstruction and technical documentation based on original Internet Force materials preserved by Marco Iannacone.
> Author and archive curator: Marco Iannacone · https://pippo.com
> License: [CC BY 4.0](../../../LICENSE)

**Hypertxt** was an authoring/documentation product (published on the Web as
*MaISoft Hypertxt*) developed by the archive author on his own personal PC, with
**Windows in a dual-boot configuration**. It was a personal project, not an
Internet Force infrastructure service, yet part of it survives precisely because
a DAT cassette of the `marco` workstation was kept and recovered in 2026.

## What survived, and why only in part

The **complete** development archive of Hypertxt lived on the Windows partition
of the author's personal PC, also because the compilers needed to produce the
WinHelp files operated in that environment. That primary archive was lost.

What survived is a partial copy on the Linux workstation `marco`: snapshots
transferred occasionally and without a regular schedule. The material present
today is therefore **fragmentary** and does not represent the complete original
development environment. The detailed provenance is in
[`PROVENANCE.md`](PROVENANCE.md) / [`PROVENANCE.en.md`](PROVENANCE.en.md).

not present reconstructed sources as if they were originals.

## Version note

`hyper140.zip`, `hyper141.zip` and `hyper14b.zip` are **byte-for-byte
identical** and contain a `FILE_ID.DIZ` declaring **Hypertxt 1.41**.
`hyper14b.zip` was already in use in March 1997 as a download name, but in the
preserved snapshot it does not contain a 1.44b release: the ZIP filename alone
is **not** proof of the version it contains.

## Important consequence

The absence of a release or source file from the Linux copy **does not imply
that it never existed** in the primary project archive. The recovered material
is part of the story, not the whole of it.
